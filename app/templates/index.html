Method: spectral subtraction

This web app is an extension of the Simulink model; the Simulink model is the primary implementation. Both implement the steps below (the “Match Simulink basic” preset reproduces the model's settings exactly). Equation numbers match docs/ALGORITHM.md.

1 · Frame, window, FFT

Hann window (periodic), N = 512, 50 % overlap: 32 ms frames at 16 kHz, short enough for speech to be roughly stationary.

2 · Estimate the noise spectrum

Mean magnitude of the frames in the first T = 0.5 s, assumed speech-free. Other modes: a selected region, a separately recorded noise profile, or a low percentile over all frames (“minimum statistics”).

3 · Subtract, with over-subtraction α and spectral floor β

α > 1 removes more than the average noise, so random noise peaks are suppressed too (less residual noise, more speech distortion). β stops bins from dropping to zero. Isolated surviving peaks are heard as musical noise, and the floor masks them with a smooth low-level background. p = 1 is magnitude subtraction (the Simulink model), p = 2 is power subtraction.

Extended options (Python only, off by default): the Wiener-style gain (Eq. 9), and smoothing of G over time and frequency (Eq. 10–11).

4 · Reconstruct with the noisy phase

The ear is fairly insensitive to phase at moderate SNR, so the noisy phase is reused. Overlap-add of the IFFT frames rebuilds the waveform. The periodic Hann window at 50 % overlap sums to exactly 1, so no extra normalisation is needed.

Metrics

With a clean reference (demo mode): SNR , SI-SDR and segmental SNR. Without one (uploads and recordings) these are shown as n/a. Never estimated. Reference-free indicators instead: noise reduction over VAD noise frames, and a rough VAD-based SNR estimate.

Limitations

  • Assumes stationary noise: babble, clicks and changing noise are only partly removed.
  • Musical noise at high α / low β; speech distortion at low SNR.
  • The initial mode needs a speech-free lead-in.
